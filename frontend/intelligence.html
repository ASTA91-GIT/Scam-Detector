THREAT MODELLING

Primary Recruitment Fraud Typologies

💳

Upfront Training & Equipment Fees

Scammers pretend to hire candidates and immediately mandate payment for proprietary training software, onboarding hardware, or background checks via Zelle, Venmo, or wire transfer.

🪙

Cryptocurrency & "Task Optimization"

Victims are recruited for "e-commerce order optimization" or "app review ratings" on Telegram. They are prompted to deposit USDT/crypto to unlock frozen balances before discovering funds cannot be withdrawn.

📦

Package Re-shipping Schemes

Advertised as "Quality Assurance Specialist" or "Logistics Coordinator". Victims receive packages bought with stolen credit cards and reship them overseas, becoming unwitting accomplices in fencing rings.

🏛️

Fake Government & Public Agency Roles

Impersonators forge official seals, offering immediate appointment letters for postal, defense, or census roles in exchange for "processing fees" or security vetting bonds.

🎓

Fake Internship Stipend Schemes

College students and recent graduates are promised guaranteed certificates and high stipends but are required to pay registration or "offer letter processing charges."

🪪

Executive & HR Impersonation

Fraudsters clone names and LinkedIn profiles of genuine corporate HR managers. They conduct superficial text interviews via Telegram or WhatsApp and harvest SSNs and banking details.

ANATOMY COMPARISON

Genuine Offer vs Fraudulent Letter

✓ Authentic Corporate Offer

SAFE PATTERNS
  • Sent from authenticated enterprise domain (e.g., talent@acme.com).
  • Preceded by multiple multi-stage video or in-person technical interviews.
  • Includes detailed benefits, formal PTO, and standard tax withholding paperwork.
  • Provides adequate evaluation period (typically 3 to 7 business days) without pressure.
  • Equipment is shipped directly by company IT; zero candidate expenditure required.

✗ Fraudulent Recruitment Scheme

DANGER SIGNALS
  • Sent from free providers (acme.recruitment@gmail.com) or lookalikes.
  • Immediate job offer extended after a quick questionnaire or no interview at all.
  • Disproportionately high compensation ($75/hr for basic entry data entry).
  • Extreme urgency ("Accept within 2 hours or position will be forfeited").
  • Requests money for equipment vendors, training licenses, or cashier check cashing.
